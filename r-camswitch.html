Research · Mechanism Design

3D-Printed Rotary Cam Switch for Steering-Mode Transitions in a Four-Wheel Independent Steering Rover

V. Shah, A. Saxena, M. S. Dasgupta. Under review at ACDSA 2027 (Int. Conf. on AI, Computer, Data Sciences and Applications).

Mechanism
3D-printed cam + 12-bit encoder
Switch window
~12°, one clean transition
Ackermann error
≤ 1.5%
Inter-module skew
≤ 50 ms

Problem

A four-wheel independent steering (4WIS) rover can turn on the spot or drive with Ackermann geometry, but switching between these modes must be reliable and verifiable, especially on an autonomous platform.

Approach

A low-cost 3D-printed rotary cam switch paired with an encoder drives and verifies the transition between zero-radius pivot and Ackermann steering. The mechanism was developed for the CRISS Robotics rover and tested on the autonomous vehicle, with a five-phase transition logic in ROS 2 that withholds drive torque until the new geometry is confirmed.

Results

  • On the bench the cam switch closed over a fixed window of about 12° of encoder-shaft rotation and produced one clean transition per crossing at a 5 ms sampling period; a threshold on the encoder alone changed state three times over the same crossing.
  • On the rover, all 30 transitions into pivot mode and 27 of 40 back to Ackermann completed, with about 10 s latency (set by the steering rate) and at most 50 ms skew between modules.
  • In Ackermann mode the measured wheel angles deviated from the ideal geometry by at most 1.5%.
